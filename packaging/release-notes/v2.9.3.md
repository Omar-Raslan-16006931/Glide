## What's new in 2.9.3

**Comparison clips in two layouts.** The *Comparison clip* tile now opens a small dialog:
- **Split**: one picture cut down the middle, the console's own frame rate on the left, Glide on the right.
- **Side by side**: the whole picture twice, each at half size, the console's frame rate on the left and Glide on the right.
- **Length**: 10, 15, 30 or 60 seconds.

**Frame-rate labels on every clip.** Both sides are labelled at the top (for example *Console · 30 FPS* and *Glide · 120 FPS*). HDR clips used to have no labels or divider line, and a label too wide for its half was left out. Both now always appear, drawn in HDR too and scaled down to fit. With XeSS frame generation Glide now says before recording that XeSS frames can't go into a comparison clip, instead of saving a clip where both sides look the same.

**Recordings open in Vegas Pro and other editors.** When a recording finishes, the MP4 is rewritten as a standard MP4, without re-encoding. While recording it stays in the crash-safe format, so a crash still keeps what was recorded.

**Sharper recordings.** The standard recording settings now use about twice the bitrate (about 37 Mbps for 1080p60, 66 Mbps for 1440p60, 150 Mbps for 4K60 with H.264). Keyframes and fast motion get more room, and flat areas such as skies get more detail. A fixed bitrate under *Settings, Playback* still applies as before.

**Tested how.** In CI on Windows:
- unit tests, including writing a fragmented MP4 like a recording, finishing it and reading it back;
- QML tests for the new dialog;
- GPU tests of both layouts in SDR and HDR on WARP (Microsoft's software D3D12 device);
- the install test.

Not yet tested with a capture card or on an RTX card: reports are welcome.
